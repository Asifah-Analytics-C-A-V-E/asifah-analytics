The DRC's money and its war sit at opposite ends of the country: copper and cobalt in the south-east (Katanga), conflict gold and coltan in the east (the Kivus, Ituri), where an Ebola epidemic now runs through the same provinces as the fighting. Geography 26 provinces · nine neighbours · 37 km of coast
The DRC is nearly the size of Western Europe. Kinshasa sits in the far west; the war and the Ebola epidemic are in the far east, on the borders with Uganda, Rwanda and Burundi, about 1,600 km away; the copper and cobalt are in the south-east, on the border with Zambia. How we got here, and 2026 so far
  • Jan – Feb 2025 M23 and the AFC took Goma (late January) and Bukavu (February). UN experts said Rwandan troops fought alongside M23; Kigali denies backing the group. (Wikipedia: M23 campaign)
  • Jun – Dec 2025 A US-brokered DRC–Rwanda peace agreement (27 Jun); a separate Qatar-facilitated DRC–M23 track in Doha (Declaration of Principles, July). On 4 Dec the Washington Accords and a US–DRC strategic minerals partnership were signed. Days later M23 entered Uvira, then announced a withdrawal under US pressure. (Al Jazeera · Critical Threats)
  • Dec 2025 The UN Security Council renewed MONUSCO to 20 Dec 2026 (Resolution 2808), with a role supporting Doha ceasefire verification. (UN News)
  • Apr – May 2026 The first major deal under the US–DRC minerals partnership (Virtus Minerals, April). M23 fully withdrew from the Ruzizi Plain in May; Burundian troops returned to Uvira. (Mongabay · Critical Threats)
  • May 2026 WHO declared the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak a public health emergency of international concern (17 May). There is no licensed vaccine for this strain. (WHO)
  • Jul – Sep 2026 Kinshasa signed a disarmament protocol with one faction of the FDLR (28 Jul); Kigali called it a "crude manipulation" that does not meet the Accords. Joint security coordination meetings in July, August and September. Uganda declared its own Ebola outbreak over (August). (Al Jazeera)
  • Oct 2026 Joseph Kabila, sentenced to death in absentia in September 2025, launched the FRN in Goma (12 Oct), folding M23 and the AFC inside it. The US sanctioned Rwanda's Ministry of Defence and Zigama Credit over alleged support for M23, which Kigali rejects (28 Oct). Ebola reached Kenya and, by one imported case, Nigeria. (Al Jazeera · Critical Threats · WHO)
  • Nov 2026 The US announced Rwanda's exclusion from AGOA from January 2027 (2 Nov). The FRN opened an offensive in Kalehe, South Kivu (6 Nov), taking Bunyakiri; the army announced a counter-offensive. WHO confirmed Ebola is still a global emergency (18 Nov): 11,613 confirmed cases and 5,678 deaths by 17 Nov. (Critical Threats · WHO)

Contested claims, shown as such: UN experts and the US government say Rwanda supports M23; Rwanda denies it and says the FDLR, which it calls a genocidal militia, fights alongside Congolese forces. This page reports both and adjudicates neither.

How to read this page

This is a sensor page. Every figure is counted by somebody else and carries that body's name and date. Where a source did not report, the page says not reporting, never zero.

The humanitarian dial is EBOLA: confirmed cases and deaths from WHO, with their date. WHO publishes no machine-readable count, so the figure is updated by hand and the date is always shown: a stale number is visibly stale.

"What the DRC depends on" is the page's one interpretive frame, and it is deliberately made of dated facts rather than scores. What those dependencies mean for stability is the rhetoric page's job.

Why the DRC matters beyond the DRC

The region. Eastern Congo is where the Great Lakes' quarrels are fought: Rwanda and the FDLR, Uganda and the ADF, Burundi and its own rebels. Each neighbour has troops or proxies inside it, and each says it is there for its own security.

The continent. An Ebola strain with no licensed vaccine, spreading in a war zone, has already crossed to Kenya and (once) to Nigeria. The disease follows the roads and the displaced.

The world. The DRC mines most of the world's cobalt and is a top-five copper producer. Chinese companies dominate the mines; the US has made a minerals partnership the centrepiece of its peace diplomacy. Whoever stabilises the east has a claim on the supply chain for batteries and electronics.

Glossary
M23 · AFC · FRN
M23: the March 23 Movement, the armed group that took Goma and Bukavu in 2025. AFC: the Congo River Alliance, its political umbrella led by Corneille Nangaa. FRN: the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, launched by Joseph Kabila on 12 Oct 2026, which absorbed the AFC/M23 structure and says it inherits its Doha commitments.
FARDC · Wazalendo
The Congolese army · "patriots": pro-government militias fighting alongside it.
FDLR
Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda: a Rwandan Hutu armed group in eastern Congo, founded by people linked to the 1994 genocide. Neutralising it is a core Washington Accords commitment.
ADF · CODECO
Allied Democratic Forces, a Ugandan-origin group pledged to Islamic State, active in Ituri and North Kivu · a Lendu militia in Ituri's Djugu territory.
Twirwaneho
A Banyamulenge self-defence militia in the South Kivu highlands (Minembwe), aligned with the FRN.
MONUSCO
The UN stabilisation mission in the DRC; mandate to 20 Dec 2026 (Resolution 2808).
Washington Accords · Doha track
The US-brokered DRC–Rwanda agreements (Jun and Dec 2025) · the Qatar-facilitated DRC–M23 (now FRN) negotiations.
Operation Shujaa
The joint Ugandan (UPDF) and Congolese army campaign against the ADF, since 2021.
ARECOMS
The DRC's strategic minerals regulator, which sets cobalt export quotas.
Bundibugyo virus
One of the Ebola species; unlike Zaire ebolavirus, it has no licensed vaccine or approved treatment.
IPC · DTM · PHEIC
The 1–5 acute food-insecurity scale · IOM's displacement counts · WHO's highest alert, a public health emergency of international concern.
