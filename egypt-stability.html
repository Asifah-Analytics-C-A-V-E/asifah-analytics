    🧭Why Egypt is the hinge
    ▼

    Three crises meet here. With Hormuz shut, Saudi crude leaves through the East-West Petroline to Yanbu, then the Red Sea and Suez. The Houthis target that route, and Egypt's parliament authorised defending Saudi Arabia on 26 Oct 2026. Egypt is also the world's largest wheat importer, buying mostly from the Black Sea.

    So what: a successful strike on the Petroline or Yanbu would cut Riyadh's exports, Egypt's canal revenue and world crude supply at once. Egypt's fiscal recovery is coupled to Saudi Arabia's bypass route.

    As of 1 Nov 2026 · Asifah scoping note (Mecca Pact & Egypt, 31 Oct 2026)
    🏛️Government & who decides
    ▼

    President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi has governed since 2014; his current term runs to 2030 under the 2019 constitutional amendments. PM Mostafa Madbouly (since 2018) survived the February 2026 reshuffle, which brought in a new Defence Minister, Gen. Ashraf Mansour, and a new chief of staff.

    How force abroad is authorised: the President must consult the National Defense Council and win a two-thirds vote of the House of Representatives. Both happened in October 2026 for the defence of Saudi Arabia, and the authorisation was framed as defensive: Sisi has said Egypt will not attack Yemen.

    Read with care: political space is narrow and the press is tightly controlled, so dissent is under-reported. That is why this page never reads the social dimension as "calm".

    As of Nov 1 2026 · The National (Feb 2026 reshuffle), New Lines / Al Jazeera (Oct 2026 vote)
    💱The pound, the IMF and Gulf money
    ▼

    The $8bn IMF Extended Fund Facility expires on 15 Dec 2026, with its final review planned for Q4 2026. The pound floated in March 2024 and has traded in roughly the 48-54 range in 2026. Gulf states hold billions in deposits at the central bank and are owed rollovers; the March 2024 Ras El-Hekma deal with the UAE was the largest single inflow.

    Watch: the final review outcome and any successor programme, deposit rollovers, the parallel-market premium, and IMF pressure to sell down military-owned firms.

    As of Oct 2026 · IMF, CBE, Ahram Online, Bloomberg

    🍞Bread: the political price
    ▼

    Baladi bread is subsidised for about 70 million people. Bread prices have historically been a trigger: the 1977 bread riots and the "bread, freedom, social justice" slogan of 2011. Mostakbal Misr, a military-linked agency, replaced GASC as the state wheat buyer in late 2024, which makes tender pricing less transparent.

    Failure mode: fiscal first, street second. A wheat shock hits the subsidy bill and the FX position before it reaches bakeries, and reaches the street only if the state passes the cost on.

    As of Jul-Oct 2026 · corridor_dependence registry, WFP, trade press
    ⚡Gas: the quiet hinge to Israel
    ▼

    Egypt went from gas exporter to importer as Zohr declined, leaving a gap of about 2 bcf/d. Israeli pipeline gas, from Leviathan and Tamar, fills part of it under a 2019 deal. A larger $35bn Leviathan deal was approved by Israel in Dec 2025. Flows stopped when the Iran war began on 28 Feb 2026 and resumed in early April. LNG imported through floating terminals covers the rest, paid in dollars.

    So what: the treaty relationship is not only Rafah and Sinai. It is also Cairo's summer power cuts and the fertilizer plants that are shut first, which links into the wheat and fertilizer webs.

    As of Apr 2026 · Ahram Online, The National, Israel Hayom
    🚢Suez & the Red Sea
    ▼

    Canal revenue collapsed after the Houthi shipping attacks began in late 2023: $10.25bn in 2023, then less than half that since. It has recovered in 2026, to $4.67bn in FY2025/26 and $567m in Aug 2026, carried by Saudi crude rerouted through Yanbu. The Houthis took Yemen's Red Sea west coast in Sep 2026 and have warned Cairo directly over the Saudi defence vote.

    As of Sep-Oct 2026 · Suez Canal Authority, New Lines, Rio Times

    🛡️Four borders, one army
    ▼
    • East: Gaza, Rafah and North Sinai, under the 1979 treaty with Israel. Egyptian troop levels in Sinai are a standing friction point.
    • South: Sudan's war; Egypt backs the SAF, and refugees cross north. Also the disputed Halaib Triangle and the Nile/GERD dispute with Ethiopia. The World Bank's extreme flag on Egypt is water stress.
    • West: Libya, where Egypt (with the UAE) backs Haftar's eastern camp.
    • Sea: the Red Sea, now including the Saudi defence commitment.
