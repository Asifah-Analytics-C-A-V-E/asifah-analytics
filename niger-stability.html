2026 so far — what changed this year
  • Jan–Feb 2026 Islamic State fighters attacked Niamey's airport and Air Base 101; Moscow confirmed Russian forces helped repel them. (Al Jazeera)
  • Mar 2026 Niger and Algeria rebuilt ties after the 2025 diplomatic rupture across the Sahel. (AllAfrica)
  • May 2026 The United States delivered about $23 million of military equipment, its first since US troops left in 2024. Niger also raised its stake in the China-built Benin export pipeline. (Al Khaleej · Bloomberg)
  • Jun 2026 Benin's new president visited Niamey; Niger set preconditions for reopening the border, shut since 2023 (a defence and non-aggression accord, and transparency on foreign forces near the border). (Africanews · Seneweb)
  • Aug 2026 Algeria sent helicopters, its first military deployment abroad since 1973. On 29–30 August soldiers mutinied in Niamey, attacking an airbase and the presidential palace; Russian forces helped end it within 24 hours and Algeria flew in Su-30 fighters. Niamey blamed France, which denied it. (AP · ADF)
  • Sep 2026 The US Development Finance Corporation approved up to $414 million for Dasa, Global Atomic's uranium project, two years after US troops left. Five more exiled critics were stripped of nationality. (Al Jazeera · HRW)
  • Nov 2026 The UN raised the conditions of former president Bazoum's detention. (Gazette)

How to read this page

This is a sensor page. Every figure is counted by somebody else and carries that body's name and date. Where a source did not report, the page says not reporting, never zero.

The humanitarian dial is HOSTING: more than 436,000 of the people displaced in Niger fled someone else's war. The second dial is expulsions from Algeria, because it is the one flow set by a neighbour's policy rather than by conditions in Niger.

"What Niger depends on" is the page's one interpretive frame, and it is deliberately made of dated facts rather than scores. What those dependencies mean for stability is the rhetoric page's job.

Why Niger matters beyond Niger

The region. Niger anchors the Alliance of Sahel States with Mali and Burkina Faso, and sits between North Africa and the Gulf of Guinea. Insurgent pressure in the Tillabéri tri-border and around Lake Chad runs south toward Benin, Togo and northern Nigeria.

The continent. Niger is the clearest case of two trends: resource nationalism (SOMAÏR nationalised, Orano pushed out) and Russia's "regime protection" offer, now joined by Algeria. The Trans-Saharan Gas Pipeline from Nigeria to Algeria needs about 1,600 km more to be built, much of it across Niger.

The world. Uranium (historically 15–25% of EU supply, and now a US critical mineral), migration to Europe through Agadez, and three great powers — Russia, the United States and China — all holding stakes in the same country at once.

The CFA franc peg — read the peg, not the tick

The West African CFA franc (XOF) is fixed to the euro at 655.957, so XOF/USD moves only as EUR/USD moves. A daily change on that tile is a reading on Frankfurt, not on Niamey.

The signal is the peg itself: AES members have raised leaving the franc zone. That would be a rupture, not a drift. (BCEAO / UEMOA)

Glossary
AES — Alliance des États du Sahel
Confederation of Niger, Mali and Burkina Faso, formed after the three juntas left ECOWAS (effective January 2025).
CNSP
Conseil national pour la sauvegarde de la patrie — the military council that took power on 26 July 2023, led by General Abdourahamane Tiani.
Africa Corps
Russia's state-run military presence in Africa, under the Ministry of Defence; it absorbed most of the former Wagner network. Based at Air Base 101, Niamey.
JNIM
Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin, the al-Qaeda-aligned coalition active in Tillabéri and across the Sahel.
IS Sahel (ISSP / ISGS)
Islamic State Sahel Province, strongest in the Tillabéri and Tahoua border zones.
ISWAP / Boko Haram
The two jihadist factions of the Lake Chad basin, active in the Diffa region.
FPL
Front Patriotique de Libération, a northern armed group that has attacked the oil pipeline.
SOMAÏR
The Arlit uranium mining company, formerly run by France's Orano; nationalised by Niger in 2025.
Dasa
Global Atomic's uranium project near Agadez, described as Africa's highest-grade deposit; US DFC financing approved September 2026.
WAPCO
West African Oil Pipeline Company — operates the ~2,000 km Agadem–Sèmè (Benin) crude line built by China's CNPC.
Point Zero
The desert drop-off about 15 km from Assamaka where Algeria leaves people it expels.
UMOA-Titres
The regional government-bond market of the eight CFA-franc states, where Niger borrows.
DTM · IPC
IOM's displacement counts (numbered rounds) · the 1–5 acute food-insecurity scale.
