⚔️ Northeast Jihadist Insurgencies — Boko Haram, ISWAP
▼

Northeast Nigeria has been at war with jihadist insurgencies since 2009. The original Boko Haram movement has split into two main factions, both still active across the Lake Chad Basin.

Key Groups:

  • JAS (Jama'atu Ahlis Sunna Lidda'awati wal-Jihad): Original Boko Haram, historically led by Abubakar Shekau (killed by ISWAP, May 2021). Weakened but still active in Sambisa Forest area.
  • ISWAP (Islamic State West Africa Province): Broke from Boko Haram 2016; now dominant faction; centered in Lake Chad islands and northern Borno; led by Abu Musab al-Barnawi and successors.
  • Ansaru: Smaller Al-Qaeda-aligned faction operating in northwestern Nigeria.

Counter-Insurgency Framework:

  • Operation Hadin Kai: Current Nigerian Army operation in the northeast (replaced Operation Lafiya Dole)
  • MNJTF (Multinational Joint Task Force): Nigeria + Cameroon + Chad + Niger + Benin coalition based in N'Djamena
  • Civilian JTF (CJTF): Local self-defense militias in Maiduguri
  • US support: US Green Berets, ISR drones, and bilateral training programs

Key Theaters: Borno State (Maiduguri, Bama, Konduga, Damasak), Yobe State (Damaturu, Buni Yadi), Adamawa State, Lake Chad islands.

🔫 Northwest Banditry & Lakurawa Emergence
▼

Northwest Nigeria is contested by armed criminal groups — collectively termed "bandits" — operating across Zamfara, Sokoto, Katsina, Kaduna, Kebbi, and Niger states.

Major Bandit Networks:

  • Bello Turji: Most-wanted bandit kingpin operating in Zamfara/Sokoto borderlands
  • Ado Aleru: Senior bandit commander; multiple military bounties
  • Kachalla networks: Multiple regional "kachallas" (commanders) running kidnap-for-ransom enterprises

Lakurawa — Emerging Group (2024-2025):

  • New armed group identified in Sokoto, Kebbi, and parts of Borno late 2024
  • Reported to be ideologically jihadist with sources in Niger/Sahel
  • Filling vacuum left by AES military departure from regional security cooperation
  • Significant concern as a potential bridge between Sahel jihadism and Nigerian banditry

Counter-Operations:

  • Operation Hadarin Daji: Nigerian Army operation against bandits in NW
  • Operation Fasan Yamma: Multi-state joint task force
  • Operation Whirl Punch: Air Force-led strike operations against bandit camps

🌾 Middle Belt — Plateau, Kaduna, Benue
▼

Nigeria's Middle Belt is the geographic seam between Muslim-majority North and Christian-majority South — and the locus of persistent farmer-herder conflict with ethnic and religious dimensions.

Key Dynamics:

  • Farmer-herder conflict: Predominantly between Fulani pastoralists (often Muslim) and settled farmers (often Christian); driven by land-use pressure, climate change, and weak governance
  • Plateau State: Recurring massacres in Mangu, Bokkos, Bassa local government areas; deaths in the thousands across 2023-2024
  • Kaduna State: Particularly Southern Kaduna — long history of intercommunal violence
  • Benue State: Major farmer-herder violence; Anti-Open Grazing Law (2017) sparked herder backlash
  • "Genocide" framing: Some Western Christian advocacy groups frame as anti-Christian genocide; reality is more complex but Christian communities have suffered disproportionately

2024 Events: December 2023 Plateau Christmas attacks (200+ killed) marked one of the worst single events; smaller attacks throughout 2024 and into 2025-26.

Government Response: President Tinubu has faced criticism for limited federal response; State Police debate ongoing as potential reform.

🛢️ Oil Economy & Niger Delta
▼

Nigeria is Africa's largest oil producer by reserves and historically by output, though OPEC+ quota and theft have driven production below 1.5M bpd in many recent months.

Key Players:

  • NNPCL (Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited): National oil company; recently restructured under PIA 2021
  • IOCs (International Oil Companies): ExxonMobil, Chevron, Shell, TotalEnergies, Eni — most divesting onshore assets to local operators
  • Indigenous independents: Seplat, Aiteo, Heirs Holdings, Sahara Group — buying IOC onshore stakes
  • Dangote Refinery: $20B private refinery (Lekki, Lagos); fully operational 2024-2026; massive geopolitical-economic shift

Niger Delta Militancy:

  • Niger Delta Avengers: Resurgent militant group threatening pipeline attacks
  • Oil theft / bunkering: Estimated 250,000-400,000 bpd stolen via illegal pipeline taps; massive corruption + community insurgency dimension
  • Tompolo / Tantita: Government's awarded pipeline-security contract to former militant leader Tompolo's company — controversial but somewhat effective
  • Amnesty programs: Niger Delta amnesty payments continue 16+ years post-2009 origin

Strategic Implications: Dangote Refinery is reducing Nigeria's perverse status as crude exporter / refined fuel importer. Has implications for FX reserves, fuel subsidy politics, and regional refined product trade.

🇺🇸 US–Nigeria Relations
▼

The US-Nigeria relationship is one of the most strategically important in Africa — based on population (Africa's largest), economy (largest in West Africa), and counter-terrorism cooperation.

Bilateral Framework:

  • US Embassy Abuja: Major diplomatic post; full bilateral agenda including security, trade, governance
  • US Consulate Lagos: Commercial and visa hub; commercial ties heavily concentrated
  • AGOA: Nigeria is AGOA-eligible; recent oil-export-driven trade volumes
  • USTDA: Trade and development infrastructure financing
  • Power Africa: Long-running initiative for electrification

Security Cooperation:

  • 1206 / 333 authorities: Section 1206/333 funding for Nigerian military training and equipment
  • US Special Forces: Periodic deployments; Green Berets trained Nigerian troops
  • Super Tucano sales: 12 A-29 Super Tucano aircraft delivered 2021-2022 for counter-Boko Haram operations
  • Christian persecution issue: US Commission on International Religious Freedom (USCIRF) recommends Nigeria for Country of Particular Concern (CPC) designation; State Department response has varied across administrations

Watch Items: AGOA renewal/expiration debates; potential CPC designation; Tinubu government engagement with Trump administration; ECOWAS standby force prospects given AES exit.
