📋Key Facts
▼
  • Capital: Manila. Population: about 113 million (2024 census).
  • Government: Presidential republic. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. (single six-year term, 2022–2028); Vice President Sara Duterte, elected separately.
  • Geography: More than 7,600 islands in three groups (Luzon, the Visayas, Mindanao), bordered by the South China Sea to the west and the Philippine Sea (Pacific) to the east. The Luzon Strait separates northern Luzon from Taiwan.
  • Alliances: U.S. treaty ally (Mutual Defense Treaty, 1951). Founding member of ASEAN; 2026 ASEAN chair.
  • U.S. travel advisory: Level 2 nationwide; Level 4 for the Sulu Archipelago and Marawi; Level 3 for most of Mindanao.

⚔️The West Philippine Sea & the Shoals
▼

Manila calls the part of the South China Sea inside its exclusive economic zone the West Philippine Sea. In 2016 an arbitral tribunal, in a case the Philippines brought under UNCLOS, found that China's nine-dash line has no legal basis. China rejects the award and patrols the area with its coast guard and maritime militia.

  • Second Thomas Shoal (Ayungin): Philippine Marines live aboard the BRP Sierra Madre, a WWII-era ship grounded there in 1999 and resupplied by sea. Resupply missions have met water cannon, ramming and, in June 2024, a boarding by Chinese coast guard personnel.
  • Scarborough Shoal (Bajo de Masinloc): Under effective Chinese control since a 2012 standoff. China declared a "nature reserve" there in 2025; Chinese vessels water-cannoned Philippine ships nearby in July 2026. It lies about 220 km west of Luzon.
  • Sabina Shoal (Escoda): Near Second Thomas; site of Philippine–Chinese vessel collisions in August 2024.
  • Thitu Island (Pag-asa): The largest Philippine-held feature in the Spratlys, with a civilian community and an airstrip.
  • Reed (Recto) Bank: Undeveloped natural gas inside the Philippine EEZ; exploration has been repeatedly suspended.
  • Mischief and Subi Reefs: Chinese-built artificial islands with runways, within the Philippine EEZ as defined by the 2016 award.

🛡️Defense, the Alliance & Partners
▼
  • Mutual Defense Treaty (1951): U.S. officials have stated that Article IV covers armed attacks on Philippine armed forces, public vessels and aircraft, including the coast guard, anywhere in the South China Sea. The treaty has never been invoked.
  • EDCA: U.S. rotational access to nine Philippine military sites, four of them added in 2023, including sites in Cagayan and Isabela in northern Luzon.
  • Missile systems: The U.S. Army's Typhon mid-range system deployed to northern Luzon in 2024 and remained; U.S. Marine NMESIS anti-ship launchers deployed to Batanes, on the Luzon Strait, for Balikatan 2026; additional Typhon deployments were announced in 2026. The Philippines operates Indian-built BrahMos anti-ship missiles.
  • Exercises: Balikatan (annual, U.S.–Philippine, with partners) and KAMANDAG; multinational joint sails ("maritime cooperative activities") in the West Philippine Sea.
  • Other agreements: Japan–Philippines Reciprocal Access Agreement (in force September 2025); visiting forces agreements with the U.S. and Australia.
  • Philippine forces: Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP), the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) and the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR), which patrol the West Philippine Sea; the AFP is midway through a multi-year modernization program.

🏛️Politics & Government
▼
  • Marcos–Duterte split: President Marcos and Vice President Sara Duterte ran together in 2022; the alliance broke down in 2024.
  • Rodrigo Duterte: The former president was arrested in Manila in March 2025 on an International Criminal Court warrant over drug-war killings and transferred to The Hague. The ICC confirmed the charges in April 2026 and sent the case to trial.
  • Sara Duterte: Impeached by the House of Representatives a second time on May 11, 2026. The Senate trial opened July 6, 2026; conviction requires 16 of 24 senators. A verdict was expected by mid-December 2026.
  • Next elections: Presidential and vice-presidential elections in May 2028.
  • ASEAN chair 2026: Manila chairs ASEAN this year and has sought to conclude a South China Sea Code of Conduct with China.

🏭Economy & Trade
▼
  • Structure: Services-led and consumption-driven. Business-process outsourcing (BPO) and remittances from overseas Filipino workers are major sources of foreign currency; remittances equal roughly 8% of GDP.
  • Overseas workers: A large share of overseas Filipino workers are employed in Gulf states, so Middle East security and oil prices both bear on household income.
  • Exports: Electronics and semiconductors (assembly, test and packaging) lead merchandise exports; nickel ore is the main mineral export.
  • Imports: Rice (mostly from Vietnam), wheat, oil and refined fuel, coal (mostly from Indonesia) and, since 2023, LNG.
  • China: A top trading partner. During the 2012 Scarborough standoff, China tightened inspections of Philippine banana imports.
  • Currency: Philippine peso (PHP); central bank is the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP).

🌴Mindanao, Bangsamoro & Internal Security
▼
  • Bangsamoro (BARMM): The Muslim autonomous region created under the 2014 peace agreement with the Moro Islamic Liberation Front. Its first parliamentary election, postponed four times, was held on September 14, 2026; no party won a majority of the 80 seats (Bangsamoro Federalist Party 31, United Bangsamoro Justice Party 30). The new parliament was due to take office on October 30, 2026.
  • Militancy: Abu Sayyaf and the Islamic State-linked groups behind the 2017 Marawi siege have been heavily degraded; the U.S. advisory still rates the Sulu Archipelago and Marawi as Do Not Travel.
  • Communist insurgency: The New People's Army is much reduced from its peak.
  • Natural hazards: One of the most typhoon- and earthquake-exposed countries in the world, with frequent disaster-driven displacement.
