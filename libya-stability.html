📚Knowledge Library

🏛️Divided Government (East–West Split)
▼

Libya has had no unified government since 2014. In the west, the UN-recognized Government of National Unity (GNU) under PM Abdulhamid Dbeibah governs from Tripoli. In the east, the House of Representatives (HoR, Tobruk) aligns with Khalifa Haftar's forces, with rival central banks and institutions.

The post-Gaddafi (2011) collapse was never resolved; the December 2021 elections failed to take place, freezing the split. UNSMIL mediates.

* Structural baseline. Sensor feed below reflects current open-source reporting, not analysis.
⚔️Haftar & the LNA
▼

Khalifa Haftar commands the self-styled Libyan National Army (LNA), controlling most of the east and south. His 2019–2020 offensive on Tripoli failed, repelled with Turkish support for the GNU.

He is backed by Egypt, the UAE, and Russia; his sons (Saddam, Khaled) have risen as power brokers — making LNA succession itself a watch item.

🪖Foreign Forces & Wagner / Africa Corps
▼

Libya is a theater for great-power and Gulf proxy competition. Russian Wagner — now rebranded "Africa Corps" — forces back Haftar/LNA, positioned around oil facilities and bases such as Al-Jufra; Libya is a key gateway for Russian influence into the Sahel.

On the other side, Turkish forces and Syrian mercenaries backstop the GNU. Foreign presence on both sides keeps the conflict frozen rather than resolved.

🛢️Oil — the Prize and the Weapon
▼

Libya holds Africa's largest proven oil reserves, managed by the National Oil Corporation (NOC). Production is repeatedly weaponized via blockades — eastern forces shutting ports and fields (the oil crescent, Sharara) to pressure Tripoli over revenue distribution, which is the core of the conflict.

🌊Migration & the Mediterranean
▼

Libya is the primary departure point on the central Mediterranean migration route to Europe. The EU funds the Libyan coast guard amid documented detention and abuse concerns — giving Tripoli (and the militias) real leverage with Europe.

📊Stability
▼

Effectively a fragmented state: two governments, a patchwork of militias, foreign forces, and recurring oil-blockade cycles. Periodic militia clashes in Tripoli punctuate an otherwise frozen standoff.
